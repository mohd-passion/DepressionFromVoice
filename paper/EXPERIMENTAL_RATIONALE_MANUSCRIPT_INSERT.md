# Experimental Rationale Manuscript Insert

The experimental sequence was designed to separate representation development, direct cross-corpus transfer, target-domain adaptation and within-corpus task analysis. DAIC-WOZ was used first because it provided the source-domain development setting, with participant-level PHQ8_Binary labels, guided-interview speech and a controlled train/development/provisional local labeled test structure. Androids Corpus was then used as the target-domain corpus because it differed from DAIC-WOZ in language, label definition and elicitation tasks, and because it included both reading and interview speech.

Phase 1 established the DAIC-WOZ methodology before any Android evaluation. Phase 1A tested a metadata-only sanity baseline and a final-layer WavLM baseline. The metadata baseline achieved ROC-AUC 0.4437 on the provisional local labeled DAIC test split, and the final-layer WavLM baseline achieved ROC-AUC 0.5195, motivating a more systematic representation search. Phase 1B evaluated WavLM layers and selected layer 9, while Phase 1C compared participant aggregation strategies and retained participant mean aggregation. These steps produced the frozen WavLM representation used later for free-speech-compatible transfer analysis.

Phase 1D and Phase 1E evaluated DAIC guided-interview timing features and fusion strategies. Timing-only features achieved stronger DAIC development performance than the WavLM-only candidate, and the frozen timing candidate later reached ROC-AUC 0.6775 on the provisional local labeled DAIC test split. However, the timing model depended on guided-interview response-latency definitions and was not directly portable to Androids Corpus. The frozen WavLM candidate achieved ROC-AUC 0.5065 on the same provisional DAIC test split, but it remained the appropriate candidate for Android transfer because it required only speech-derived WavLM embeddings.

Phase 2A audited Androids Corpus before modeling. This step verified that WavLM preprocessing could be reproduced for Android interview and reading speech, while direct transfer of the DAIC timing model was not valid because the required response-latency features were unavailable. Phase 2B then applied the frozen DAIC WavLM classifier zero-shot to Android interview speech. The model achieved ROC-AUC 0.6412, indicating limited ranking transfer, but both the default and frozen DAIC thresholds predicted all Android participants as negative. This result motivated a diagnosis of score scale, threshold and classifier-boundary shift.

Phase 2C separated these explanations. The frozen DAIC reference retained ROC-AUC 0.6412. Threshold-only adaptation used Android training-fold labels to move the cutoff while preserving the frozen score ranking, so it was target-domain threshold adaptation rather than pure zero-shot evaluation. Frozen-score calibration improved Brier score to 0.2474 but reduced ROC-AUC to 0.5694, indicating that calibration did not improve ranking. In contrast, an Android-trained interview classifier using the same frozen WavLM representation achieved ROC-AUC 0.8675. Together, these results indicated that the representation retained useful target-domain information, but the DAIC classifier boundary and probability scale did not transfer directly.

Phase 2D extended the analysis to Android reading speech. Frozen DAIC zero-shot reading evaluation achieved ROC-AUC 0.5795 with all-negative thresholded predictions, while an Android-trained reading classifier achieved ROC-AUC 0.8796 with a 95 percent confidence interval of [0.8113, 0.9377]. This repeated the same broad pattern seen in interview speech: direct zero-shot threshold transfer was limited, but the frozen representation supported strong within-Android discrimination when a target-domain classifier was learned.

Phase 2E then tested reading-interview transfer under participant-coherent folds. Matched reading and matched interview references achieved ROC-AUCs of 0.9048 and 0.9065. Reading-to-interview and interview-to-reading transfer achieved ROC-AUCs of 0.8475 and 0.8501, supporting shared representation information across tasks while showing task-sensitive classifier boundaries. Pooled reading-and-interview training produced the highest point estimates, 0.9058 for reading and 0.9135 for interview, but gain intervals included zero, so pooled-task results are interpreted as suggestive rather than conclusive.

Overall, the staged experiments support a cautious interpretation: frozen WavLM features retained depression-related ranking information across corpora and tasks, but decision boundaries and probability scales were corpus- and task-sensitive. The results support research claims about representation transfer, domain shift and cross-task behavior. They do not establish clinical validation, diagnostic use, screening utility or deployment readiness.
